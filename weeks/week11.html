WEEK 11 · TIME SERIES

Time-Series Analysis with Pandas

Time-series observations have a time order. Splitting, feature construction and evaluation must preserve that order to avoid using future information to predict the past.

SECTION 01

Datetime index and resampling

  • Parse timestamps into datetime values, sort chronologically and set a meaningful time index.
  • Resampling changes frequency, for example aggregating daily records into monthly totals or averages.
  • Distinguish upsampling from downsampling and decide how to handle empty time bins.
SECTION 02

Trend, seasonality and residuals

  • Trend is a longer-term direction; seasonality is a pattern that repeats at a known frequency; residuals are what remains after components are modelled.
  • Classical decomposition can represent a series as additive components or multiplicative components when variation scales with the level.
  • STL uses LOESS-based smoothing and can handle evolving seasonal patterns more flexibly in many cases.
SECTION 03

Stationarity

  • A weakly stationary series has stable mean and variance over time and autocovariance that depends on lag rather than absolute time.
  • Trend, changing variance and seasonality can violate stationarity. Visual inspection is useful but does not prove stationarity.
SECTION 04

Transformations

  • First differencing uses xₜ − xₜ₋₁ and can remove a changing level. Seasonal differencing compares observations one seasonal period apart.
  • Log transformations can reduce multiplicative variation when values are positive and the interpretation is appropriate.
  • After transformation, inspect the resulting series again. Do not apply transformations automatically without understanding their effect.
SECTION 05

Forecasting discipline

  • Use chronological train/validation/test splits or rolling-origin evaluation.
  • Construct lagged and rolling features using past data only; ensure rolling windows do not include the target time's future information.
  • Compare against a naive forecast, such as the last observed value or a seasonal-naive baseline.
SECTION 06

Practice

  • Resample timestamped observations to a monthly frequency.
  • Explain the difference between trend and seasonality.
  • Show how first differencing changes a series and why random shuffling is usually inappropriate.
