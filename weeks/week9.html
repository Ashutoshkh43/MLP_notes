WEEK 9 · UNSUPERVISED LEARNING

Unsupervised Learning & Clustering

Clustering groups observations based on similarity without using a supplied target label. The resulting groups require interpretation; they are not automatically meaningful categories.

SECTION 01

K-means

  • Choose k initial centroids, assign each observation to its nearest centroid, update centroids and repeat until convergence or an iteration limit.
  • K-means minimizes within-cluster squared distances. It favours compact, roughly spherical clusters and can be sensitive to scaling, initialization and outliers.
  • Use feature scaling when units differ. Try multiple initializations and compare plausible k values.
SECTION 02

Choosing k

  • The elbow method plots within-cluster sum of squares against k and looks for diminishing returns.
  • Silhouette score compares within-cluster cohesion with separation from the nearest other cluster; interpret it with domain context.
  • No single score proves that clusters are useful for a downstream purpose.
SECTION 03

Hierarchical agglomerative clustering

  • Start with each observation as its own cluster and repeatedly merge clusters according to a linkage rule.
  • Single, complete, average and Ward linkage define inter-cluster distance differently.
  • A dendrogram visualizes the merge hierarchy; a cut level produces a chosen grouping.
SECTION 04

Preparation and interpretation

  • Scale features when the distance calculation would otherwise be dominated by units or magnitude.
  • Visualize cluster summaries and compare feature distributions. Avoid naming a cluster solely from one arbitrary statistic.
  • Clustering on a projection such as PCA can aid visualization, but projection may hide structure in omitted dimensions.
SECTION 05

Practice

  • Explain the K-means update loop.
  • Compare K-means with hierarchical clustering.
  • Describe why a high silhouette score does not alone establish business usefulness.
